SystemVerilog testbench clock generator

Generate independent simulation clocks with an explicit time resolution. Each output accepts either frequency or period and its own high-time percentage.

Calculator · Method · References

Calculator

Timing and enable behavior

For period T and duty fraction D, high time = T × D and low time = T × (1 − D). Each duration is rounded to the nearest selected precision tick, with half-tick values rounded up. Both phases must remain at least one tick long. The result reports the realized frequency and duty cycle after this rounding.

All outputs start low. The enable is checked before each rising edge. Disabling during a high phase lets that pulse complete, after which the output remains low. Re-enabling starts a complete low phase. This avoids truncated high pulses in the generated simulation source.

Input format

Use one to sixteen pipe-separated rows. Frequency units are Hz, kHz, MHz, and GHz. Period units are s, ms, us, ns, ps, and fs. Names must be unique, valid SystemVerilog identifiers. Duty cycle must be strictly between 0% and 100%.

Worked example

A 50 MHz clock at 30% duty has a 20 ns period, a 6 ns high phase, and a 14 ns low phase. At 1 ps precision, those durations are 6,000 and 14,000 ticks.

Simulation and hardware

The generated initial loops and delay controls are simulation sources. For FPGA hardware, use the clock-enable divider or the device’s dedicated clocking resources. Compile the clock module and example testbench together with SystemVerilog support enabled.
