Expression syntax

Supported arithmetic is +, −, *, /, %, and ^ (or ** for powers), with parentheses and scientific notation such as 1e-3. Powers associate right to left; −2^2 means −(2²). Multiplication is explicit: use 2*x. Constants are pi and e. Legacy inputs such as Math.sin(x) and Math.PI are accepted for the supported names.

Functions include sin, cos, tan, asin, acos, atan, atan2(y,x), abs, sqrt, exp, log (natural logarithm), ln, log10, floor, ceil, round, sign, min(a,b), max(a,b), and pow(a,b). The sequence functions are u(x), equal to 1 for x ≥ 0 and 0 otherwise; delta(x), equal to 1 only at x = 0; and sinc(x) = sin(πx)/(πx), with sinc(0) = 1.

The coordinate is x = start + n·Δx, with integer n from 0 through count − 1. Non-real, undefined and out-of-range results are omitted and counted. Stems and markers show individual samples. Components are limited to magnitude 10150; calculations use double-precision floating point. The expression parser accepts only the documented arithmetic and functions.

Least-squares method

For y = mx + b, the fitted slope is m = Σ(x − x̄)(y − ȳ) / Σ(x − x̄)² and the intercept is b = ȳ − mx̄. The fit minimizes the sum of squared vertical residuals. R² = 1 − Σ(y − ŷ)² / Σ(y − ȳ)² measures how much of the observed variation the fitted line explains.

A fit requires two or more points and at least two distinct x values. R² is undefined for constant y because its denominator is zero. A high R² alone does not establish causation or justify extrapolating the line beyond the observed range. The fitted line is drawn over the entered x range.

Worked examples

To plot a three-sample pulse, enter u(x)-u(x-3) with start 0, step 1, and count 5. The samples at x = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 are 1, 1, 1, 0, 0.

For a linear-fit check, enter 0,1; 1,3; 2,5. All three points lie on y = 2x + 1, so the fitted slope is 2, the intercept is 1, and R² is 1.
