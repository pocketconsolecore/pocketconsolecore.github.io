Phasor convention

For a cosine-reference phasor Z = A∠φ, the real waveform is v(t) = Apeak cos(2πft + φ). This is the real part of Z·ej2πft. Selecting a sine reference instead displays Apeak sin(2πft + φ), the imaginary projection. A cosine at phase φ is equivalent to a sine at phase φ + 90°.

For a pure sinusoid with f > 0, Apeak = √2 ARMS. Rectangular inputs first convert using A = hypot(real, imaginary) and φ = atan2(imaginary, real). Positive phase advances the waveform. At f = 0, the selected formula produces a constant value over one second; the RMS conversion describes the sinusoidal amplitude parameter and is not the RMS of that DC value.

Zero magnitude produces a zero waveform and an undefined phasor angle; zero is used as the rendering phase. Frequency is limited to 1012 Hz and plots to 20 cycles to keep sampling legible. The graph is a numerical visualization of the formula, with eight significant digits in the table.

Worked example

A 10 V RMS phasor at 30° and 60 Hz becomes v(t) = 10√2 cos(120πt + π/6) V with a cosine reference. Its peak amplitude is approximately 14.1421 V, its period is 1/60 s, and its value at t = 0 is approximately 12.2474 V.
