    Method and conventions

    A phasor is a complex number z = a + jb = r(cos θ + j sin θ), where j² = −1. Its magnitude is √(a² + b²). The principal phase uses atan2(b, a), which preserves the quadrant. The zero vector has zero magnitude and undefined phase.

    Addition and subtraction act on corresponding rectangular components. Multiplication adds phases and multiplies magnitudes. Division subtracts phases and divides magnitudes; a zero denominator is undefined. For AC circuit analysis, add phasors only when they describe the same frequency and use the same peak/RMS and sine/cosine convention.

    Multiplying two phasors here performs complex arithmetic. Reconstructing a product of time-domain sinusoids requires the appropriate trigonometric identities. Use the sinusoid plotter to see the time-domain waveform for a selected reference.

    Inputs support finite components or magnitudes up to 10150. Calculations use double-precision floating point; displayed values use eight significant digits. Very small values can underflow, and a result outside the finite range produces an error.

    Worked example

    Divide 3 + j4 by 1 − j2. Multiply the numerator and denominator by the denominator’s conjugate, 1 + j2. The numerator becomes −5 + j10 and the denominator becomes 5, giving −1 + j2. Its magnitude is √5, approximately 2.236068, and its phase is approximately 116.565051°.
